## Overview

Logistics administration is the operational backbone that keeps goods, information,
and money moving in sync across a supply chain. A large share of the role is
**documentation** (creating, verifying, and filing records) and **reporting**
(aggregating those records into decisions and compliance evidence).

This page catalogs the most **recurring** high-level tasks, grouped by function,
with the typical cadence at which each is performed. It is intended as a reference
map — not a procedure manual — for anyone scoping a logistics admin role, designing
templates, or planning automation.

<Note>
  "Recurring" here means the task repeats predictably — per shipment, per shift,
  daily, weekly, monthly, or per audit cycle — rather than as a one-off project.
</Note>

## 1. Order and shipment documentation

The most frequent documentation work is tied to the lifecycle of an individual order
or shipment. These are produced **per transaction**, so volume scales directly with
throughput.

| Task | Typical cadence | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Raise / confirm purchase orders (POs) | Per order | Authorize and record procurement |
| Generate packing lists | Per shipment | Itemize contents for handling and receipt |
| Prepare bills of lading (BOL) / air waybills (AWB) | Per shipment | Legal contract of carriage + title document |
| Issue delivery notes / dispatch notes | Per delivery | Record what left the facility |
| Capture proof of delivery (POD / ePOD) | Per delivery | Evidence of receipt; unlocks invoicing |
| Match PO ↔ shipment ↔ invoice (3-way match) | Per transaction | Prevent overpayment and fraud |

**Documentation focus:** accuracy of quantities, SKUs, weights, and addresses;
version control when orders change; and maintaining an auditable link between the PO,
the physical movement, and the eventual invoice.

## 2. Inventory and warehouse records

Warehouse administration generates a steady stream of records that feed both
operations and finance.

- **Goods-in / receiving records** — logging deliveries against expected POs,
  noting shortages, overages, and damages (SOD reports). *(Per inbound load)*
- **Goods-out / dispatch records** — confirming picks, packs, and loads. *(Per outbound load)*
- **Cycle counts and stock takes** — reconciling physical stock to system records.
  *(Daily cycle counts; periodic/annual full counts)*
- **Stock adjustment / discrepancy reports** — documenting and approving corrections.
  *(As they occur, reviewed weekly/monthly)*
- **Bin / location and putaway logs** — maintaining slotting accuracy. *(Continuous)*

<Tip>
  Inventory accuracy reporting (system stock vs. physical stock, expressed as a
  percentage) is one of the most requested recurring reports because it directly
  affects order promising, working capital, and audit outcomes.
</Tip>

## 3. Transport and carrier administration

- **Freight booking and confirmation records** — capturing carrier, service level,
  rate, and reference numbers. *(Per shipment)*
- **Load manifests and route sheets** — what is on each vehicle and in what sequence.
  *(Per departure / per route)*
- **Driver, vehicle, and hours logs** — compliance with working-time and vehicle
  inspection rules. *(Daily)*
- **Carrier performance tracking** — on-time collection/delivery, damage rates,
  failed deliveries. *(Weekly / monthly)*
- **Track-and-trace and exception logging** — recording delays, reroutes, and
  their causes. *(Continuous / daily)*

## 4. Customs, trade, and regulatory compliance

For any cross-border movement, compliance documentation is both recurring and
high-risk (errors cause fines, seizures, and delays).

| Document / task | When |
| --- | --- |
| Commercial invoice & packing list for customs | Per international shipment |
| Customs declarations (import/export entries) | Per international shipment |
| Certificates of origin / preferential trade docs | Per shipment (where applicable) |
| HS/tariff code classification and validation | Per new product / per shipment |
| Dangerous goods (DG/ADR/IMDG) documentation | Per hazardous shipment |
| Licenses, permits, and restricted-party screening | Per applicable shipment |
| Duty, tax, and tariff record-keeping | Ongoing; retained for audit |

**Reporting focus:** maintaining a defensible audit trail, tracking declaration
accuracy and error rates, and monitoring duty/VAT liabilities.

## 5. Financial, invoicing, and cost administration

- **Freight invoice receipt and audit** — checking carrier invoices against agreed
  rates and booked services. *(Per invoice; batched weekly)*
- **Freight cost reconciliation and coding** — allocating costs to the right
  cost centers, customers, or jobs. *(Weekly / monthly)*
- **Accruals and month-end close support** — estimating unbilled logistics costs.
  *(Monthly)*
- **Claims documentation** — damage, loss, and shortage claims against carriers or
  insurers. *(As incidents occur)*
- **Credit/debit note and dispute handling** — resolving billing discrepancies.
  *(Ongoing)*

## 6. Performance and management reporting

Reporting is where documentation is turned into decisions. The recurring reports
below are the ones most logistics admins own or contribute to.

### Common recurring reports and their cadence

| Report | Typical cadence | What it answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OTIF (On-Time In-Full) | Daily / weekly | Are we delivering completely and on time? |
| Order cycle time / lead time | Weekly | How long from order to delivery? |
| Inventory accuracy & stock levels | Daily / weekly | Do records match reality? Are we over/understocked? |
| Cost per shipment / cost per unit | Monthly | What is it costing us to move goods? |
| Carrier / supplier scorecards | Monthly | Which partners are reliable and cost-effective? |
| Exception & incident report | Daily | What went wrong and why? |
| Fill rate / backorder report | Weekly | How much demand did we satisfy? |
| Damage / loss / claims summary | Monthly | Where are we losing product and money? |
| Dock-to-stock & throughput | Weekly | How fast are we processing inbound/outbound? |
| SLA / KPI compliance dashboard | Monthly | Are we meeting contractual commitments? |

<Info>
  Most of these consolidate into a **monthly logistics performance pack** for
  management, plus a lighter **daily/weekly operational report** for the floor.
  The underlying data comes almost entirely from the transactional documentation
  in sections 1–5.
</Info>

### Frequently tracked KPIs

- On-time delivery / OTIF
- Order accuracy and fill rate
- Inventory accuracy and turnover
- Freight cost as a percentage of sales
- Cost per order / per shipment / per unit
- Damage and return rates
- Warehouse throughput and productivity
- Carrier and supplier on-time performance

## 7. Records management and audit support

Underlying everything is the discipline of keeping records findable and defensible.

- **Document filing and archiving** — organizing PODs, customs docs, and invoices
  for retrieval. *(Continuous)*
- **Retention and retrieval** — meeting legal retention periods (often multiple
  years) and pulling records on request. *(Ongoing / per audit)*
- **Audit preparation** — assembling evidence for internal, customer, customs, or
  financial audits. *(Per audit cycle)*
- **Master data upkeep** — keeping product, address, carrier, and rate data clean,
  since bad master data corrupts every downstream document and report. *(Ongoing)*

## Where documentation and reporting connect

The through-line across all of these tasks: **every report is only as good as the
documentation feeding it.** A clean 3-way match, an accurate POD, and correct
master data are what make an OTIF number or a cost-per-shipment figure trustworthy.
When scoping templates or automation, prioritize the transactional documents in
sections 1–5 first — they are the highest-volume, highest-recurrence, and
highest-leverage work, and they are the source of every management report in
section 6.
