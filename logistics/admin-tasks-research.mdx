## Scope

This page deliberately **excludes** the service-critical work (customs declarations,
bills of lading, dispatch, PODs) — anything where skipping it stops trucks, blocks a
shipment, or breaks the flow of goods.

Instead it catalogs the **non-essential, deferrable tasks**: the small recurring work
done by **continuous-improvement people**, **global-level management**, and
**reporting/analytics** roles. These raise performance over time but can be paused
for a day, batched, or rescheduled **without interrupting the service**.

<Note>
  **The test for this page:** if the task is skipped today, does anything stop moving?
  If **no**, it belongs here. If **yes**, it's operational and belongs in the
  service-critical layer, not here.
</Note>

## Why treat these as a separate class

- They are **discretionary** — good candidates for quiet periods, overflow time, or automation.
- They are **cumulative** — value comes from consistency over months, not from any single instance.
- They are **safe to defer** — no customer, carrier, or regulator is blocked when they slip.
- They are the first things dropped under pressure, which is exactly why naming and
  protecting them matters.

## 1. Continuous improvement (CI / Kaizen) — small recurring tasks

The bread-and-butter of a CI person: lots of small, repeatable actions that compound.
None of these are on the critical path of moving a shipment.

| Task | Typical cadence | Non-essential because… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gemba walk (go-see the floor) | Daily / weekly | Observation; operations run whether or not you walk today |
| Collect & triage staff improvement ideas | Weekly | The suggestion backlog can wait a day |
| 5S audit / spot check | Weekly | Housekeeping score, not a shipment blocker |
| Waste (muda) identification log | Weekly | Documenting waste doesn't stop the line |
| 5 Whys / root-cause on minor recurring issues | Per issue, batched | Analysis follows the incident, not the flow |
| Update SOPs & work instructions | Monthly / as needed | Current version keeps working until updated |
| Standardize a best practice across a cell/site | Monthly | Improvement, not a dependency |
| Maintain the improvement (Kaizen) idea pipeline | Weekly | A tracking board, not a live system |
| A3 / small-problem write-ups | Per project | Documentation of learning |
| Refresh a value stream map | Quarterly | Planning artifact |
| Lessons-learned / post-incident notes | Per incident | Retrospective, done after service is restored |
| Micro-metrics review for opportunities | Weekly | Looking for gains, not keeping lights on |

<Tip>
  A useful mental model: CI tasks are **"go-see, log, suggest, standardize."** All four
  are observational or advisory — they change the *next* cycle, never block the *current* one.
</Tip>

## 2. Global-level management — recurring non-operational tasks

Global/regional management work is mostly **synthesis, governance, and alignment** —
consolidating what already happened and setting direction. It sits above daily
operations, so almost none of it is service-critical.

- **Consolidate regional reports into a global view** — roll-up, not data entry. *(Monthly)*
- **Cross-region / cross-site benchmarking** — compare and rank sites. *(Monthly / quarterly)*
- **Business review cadence (MBR / QBR) prep and chairing** — governance rhythm. *(Monthly / quarterly)*
- **Best-practice sharing across regions** — spread what works. *(Monthly)*
- **Strategic KPI / target setting and review** — goals, not gates. *(Quarterly)*
- **Partner / carrier relationship reviews (QBRs)** — scorecards and roadmaps. *(Quarterly)*
- **Capacity & demand outlook reviews** — forward-looking planning. *(Monthly)*
- **Risk register review** — maintain the watch-list. *(Monthly / quarterly)*
- **Sustainability / ESG tracking** — emissions, packaging, reporting commitments. *(Monthly / quarterly)*
- **Policy, playbook & governance updates** — keep the rulebook current. *(As needed)*
- **Capability / talent development tracking** — team growth. *(Quarterly)*

<Info>
  These are **cadence-driven**, not **event-driven**. A missed QBR or a late benchmark
  is an inconvenience; it does not stop a single order from shipping. That is precisely
  what makes them the deferrable, "improvement and alignment" layer.
</Info>

## 3. Reporting & analytics — the non-essential slice

Not all reporting is equal. **Operational reports that gate a decision** (e.g. a load
plan) are essential. The tasks below are the **analytical / housekeeping** slice —
they improve insight and report quality but don't block the service if delayed.

| Task | Typical cadence | Why it's deferrable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dashboard maintenance & enhancement | Weekly / monthly | The existing dashboard still works |
| Ad-hoc / deep-dive analysis | On request | By definition, not routine-critical |
| Trend & variance commentary (the "so what") | Monthly | Narrative around numbers already published |
| Automate a manual report | Project-based | Manual version keeps running meanwhile |
| Data-quality cleanup in reporting layer | Ongoing, batched | Improves accuracy over time |
| Report rationalization (retire unused reports) | Quarterly | Housekeeping / decluttering |
| Scorecard & review-pack preparation | Monthly / quarterly | Feeds a governance meeting, not operations |
| Visualization / template improvements | As needed | Cosmetic and clarity gains |
| Backfill / historical trend building | Project-based | Analytical enrichment |

## What "non-essential" does *not* mean

<Warning>
  Non-essential ≠ low-value. These tasks are where **most long-term cost and quality
  gains** actually come from. "Non-essential" here strictly means **not service-blocking**
  — safe to defer in the short term, dangerous to neglect over the long term.
</Warning>

## How to use this list

Because everything here is deferrable, it is ideal for:

- **Filling quiet periods** — pull from this backlog when operational load is low.
- **Automation targets** — reporting housekeeping and roll-ups are prime candidates.
- **Protecting improvement time** — name and schedule these so they aren't perpetually
  crowded out by firefighting.
- **Delegation** — low-risk enough to hand to developing team members.

The single distinguishing rule for this entire page: **nothing here, if skipped today,
stops the service.**
